### 4.6 MANDATORY CORE CAPABILITY A – UNLIMITED DYNAMIC MODULE BUILDER

This is a foundational platform capability, not an optional feature. Build a fully database-driven module builder that allows authorized administrators to create, configure, publish, revise, archive and reuse an unlimited number of industries, modules and document categories without changing application source code.

Required hierarchy:
- Global EHS Library
- Industry
- Industry segment / sub-industry
- Parent module
- Child module
- Sub-child module
- Further nested levels without a fixed depth limit
- Document category
- Document / template / checklist / record

Functional requirements:
1. Create unlimited custom industries, including an “Other / Custom Industry” option.
2. Create unlimited parent, child, sub-child and deeper nested modules.
3. Add, edit, rename, duplicate, move, reorder, archive, restore and deactivate nodes.
4. Support drag-and-drop hierarchy management and bulk import/export.
5. Link each node to one or more industries, countries, states, sites, departments, activities, hazards, legal requirements, ISO standards, templates and workflows.
6. Allow a document or template to be linked to multiple modules without duplicating the underlying record.
7. Support reusable global templates and organization-specific/site-specific overrides.
8. Provide configurable fields, forms, metadata, tags, owners, access rules, review cycles and approval routes at each level.
9. Maintain immutable audit history for hierarchy changes, including who changed what and when.
10. Prevent accidental deletion of referenced records; use archive/deactivate and dependency checks.
11. Support versioning, draft/published states, effective dates and rollback.
12. Provide search, filters, favorites, recently used modules and module-level analytics.
13. Enforce tenant isolation and role-based create/edit/publish permissions.
14. Never hardcode a maximum hierarchy depth or fixed industry list in the application.

Suggested data model:
- modules: id, tenant_id, name, code, description, module_type, parent_id, hierarchy_path, depth, sort_order, status, version, created_by, updated_by, timestamps.
- industries and industry_modules: industry definitions and many-to-many module mapping.
- module_links: polymorphic links to activities, sites, departments, documents, templates, legal requirements, standards and workflows.
- module_custom_fields and module_form_schemas: configurable metadata and forms.
- module_permissions: role/user-level permissions.
- module_versions and module_audit_logs: history, approvals and rollback metadata.

Use a relational adjacency-list model with parent_id and recursive queries, optionally supplemented by a materialized path or closure table for efficient hierarchy traversal. The chosen design must support arbitrary depth and preserve referential integrity.

Acceptance tests:
- Create a new industry without code changes.
- Create at least 8 nested module levels and confirm there is no application-imposed depth limit.
- Move a child module and confirm all linked documents and permissions remain valid.
- Reuse one approved template in multiple industry modules.
- Confirm tenant A cannot view or modify tenant B modules.
- Confirm every structural change appears in the audit history.

#### India – Factories Act / OSHWC transition and applicability control

Treat the Factories Act, 1948 as a legacy and transition reference. Do not automatically assume it is the sole current governing law, nor automatically mark it repealed or inapplicable for every site.

For every Indian state/site, the engine must verify and record:
- Relevant commencement notifications for the OSHWC Code, 2020 and applicable provisions.
- Applicable Central Rules, State Rules, draft/final rules, notifications, orders and amendments.
- State-specific implementation status and effective dates.
- Repeal, savings, transition and continuing-effect provisions affecting the Factories Act, 1948 and State Factories Rules.
- Whether legacy licences, registrations, approvals, notices, forms, registers and pending proceedings continue, require migration or need fresh action.
- Relevant establishment/factory definitions, worker thresholds, hazardous-process provisions and other applicability criteria, as legally verified.
- Corresponding legacy Factories Act / State Factories Rules provisions and their successor or mapped OSHWC provisions, where an authoritative mapping exists.
- Competent authority, filing/renewal requirements, statutory records, inspection requirements and deadlines, only where supported by an official source.

Create a provision-level transition matrix with fields:
Legacy Act / Rule / Section | New Code / Rule / Section | State / Jurisdiction | Transition Type (continued / replaced / modified / repealed / savings / pending verification) | Commencement Date | Operational Impact | Required Action | Official Source | Verification Status | Reviewer.

If official commencement, final State Rules or a provision-level successor cannot be confirmed, mark it “Pending Official Verification” and do not infer legal effect. Allow legal experts and authorized administrators to update and approve the mapping.

#### Edition-wise standards and codes control

For ISO, OSHA, NFPA, NBC, CEA, SEMI and other technical references, store:
- Standard / regulation identifier and exact title
- Issuing body / authority
- Edition, publication year and amendment/corrigendum
- Publication, effective, withdrawal and supersession dates
- Jurisdiction and adoption status
- Applicable industry, activity and equipment
- Clause / section mapping only when verified and lawfully available
- Official source / licensed catalogue reference
- Copyright/licensing status
- Verification date, verifier and status

Do not assume the latest published edition is automatically the legally adopted edition in a jurisdiction. Do not reproduce copyrighted ISO/NFPA/SEMI text without appropriate licence. Store metadata and authorized references, and link to licensed copies where available.

#### Compliance engine output

For each site/activity, generate:
- Potentially applicable legal register
- Applicability rationale and evidence gaps
- Legal obligations and responsible owners
- Mandatory licence / consent / approval register
- Required documents and records
- Statutory inspection / testing / reporting calendar where verified
- Mapped ISO / OSHA / NFPA / NBC references with edition status
- Gap assessment and corrective-action plan
- Change-impact report when a law or standard is revised

Use statuses such as Confirmed Applicable, Potentially Applicable, Not Applicable (with rationale), Pending Official Verification, Superseded, and Under Legal Review. Keep legal applicability status separate from site compliance status.
